# Finding your way around
<!-- group: Start here -->

Alpymist is a tiling desktop. Windows are not dragged about and stacked; each
new one takes a share of the screen beside the others, and you move between
them and between workspaces with the keyboard. It takes a day to get used to,
and after that a stack of overlapping windows feels like a mess.

## The four keys to learn first

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Space</kbd> | Opens the menu. Type to search it. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Return</kbd> | Opens a terminal. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>B</kbd> | Opens the web browser. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>W</kbd> | Closes the window with the focus. |

## The menu

<kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Space</kbd>, or a click on the mark at the left end of
the bar, opens the menu. It holds everything: applications, screenshots,
settings, installing and removing software, updates, and the power options.

You rarely walk through it. Type a few letters and it searches every entry in
every submenu at once, applications and individual settings included:
"natural" finds Settings › Touchpad › Natural scrolling. Arrows move,
<kbd>Enter</kbd> opens, <kbd>Esc</kbd> goes back or closes. The same key that
opened the menu closes it again.

[The menu](05-the-menu.md) lists what is in it.

## Windows

A new window splits the space of the one that had the focus. The window under
the pointer has the focus, and its border is drawn in the accent colour.

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> | Moves the focus to the window in that direction. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> | Takes the window to the screen on that side. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>F</kbd> | Makes the window fill the screen, and back. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>F</kbd> | Floats the window above the tiled ones, and back. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>W</kbd> | Closes it. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd> and drag with the left button | Moves a window. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd> and drag with the right button | Resizes it. |

Some windows float in the middle of the screen by themselves, because they
are dialogs rather than somebody's work: Settings, and a terminal that a menu
entry opened for one task.

## Workspaces

A workspace is a screenful of windows. There are nine, and the bar shows the
first five, and any others in use, at its left end.

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>1</kbd> … <kbd>9</kbd> | Goes to that workspace. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>1</kbd> … <kbd>9</kbd> | Takes the focused window there. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Tab</kbd> | Shows all nine side by side. |

<kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Tab</kbd> is for when you have forgotten where the
browser went: the nine workspaces as they are now, small, three by three.
Click one to go to it, or press the key again to stay where you were.

A click on a workspace in the bar goes to it. With more than one screen,
each screen has its own nine, and the keys act on the screen the pointer is
on; see [Screens](09-screens.md).

## The bar

Along the top: the menu and the workspaces on the left, the clock in the
middle, and on the right the things you glance at, most of which open
something when clicked. [The top bar](06-the-top-bar.md) goes through them.

## Leaving

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>L</kbd> | Locks the screen. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Esc</kbd> | Opens the System menu: lock, suspend, log out, restart, shut down. |
| <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>E</kbd> | Logs out at once, without asking. |

The power icon at the right end of the bar opens the same System menu.
